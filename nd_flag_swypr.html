The Problem

A seal is for documents. A flag is for distance.

North Dakota's current flag asks people to read a seal. That works close up, but it collapses into detail at distance. A flag should still carry its identity from the road, a stadium seat, or a social thumbnail.

Current flag: meaningful up close, but visually crowded far away.

The Backstory

The Principle

The simpler the flag, the stronger the read.

A flag should use shape and color before anyone has time to read fine detail. The distance test keeps each image the same size. Only the blur changes, and that blur tells us what still survives as a symbol.

That is the question for North Dakota: after detail turns to haze, what still feels like North Dakota?

Closing Thought

Support the direction, even if you do not support one single design.

The bigger goal is public discussion around what is a flag versus what is a seal. A flag should be identifiable from a distance and use color to tell a story. This swipe story is a starting point for that debate, not the end of it.
